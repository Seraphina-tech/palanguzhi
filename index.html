THE CHALLENGE
Fading Heritage

Traditional games are rapidly losing ground to digital entertainment, threatening a 2,000-year-old cultural legacy.

Complex Rules

The intricate mechanics of sowing and capturing beads create a steep learning curve for modern, unassisted players.

Lack of Engagement

Without interactive feedback, younger generations struggle to maintain focus during long, traditional gameplay sessions.

Missed Cognition

The mental math and strategic benefits are lost when children abandon the game due to frustration or rule confusion.

UNDER THE WOOD
Core Processor

ESP32 microcontroller managing state logic, I/O processing, and display rendering at high speeds.

Sensor Matrix

Precision IR bead counters embedded cleanly in the 14 primary pits for exact physical state mapping.

Layered Architecture

Addressable RGB LEDs paired with a high-contrast IPS LCD module integrated flush with the wood grain.

Designing with the Masters

A deep dive into our field research with "Amma" - translating generations of physical, tactile knowledge into digital sensor parameters.

Live Session: Rule Verification

Tactile Authenticity

Amma emphasized that the 'feel' of the seeds and the sound they make when dropped is just as important as the rules. This directly influenced our decision to use real cowrie shells and carved rosewood.

The 'Sowing' Rhythm

Watching her demonstrate the sowing motion revealed subtle hand movements and pacing that a manual could never capture. We designed the LED guidance to mimic her exact physical rhythm.

Preserving Communal Play

The interview revealed that Pallanguzhi was historically a deeply communal game. Our 'Dual-Player Mode' was designed specifically to preserve this face-to-face social interaction.
